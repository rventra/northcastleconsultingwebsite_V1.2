Medical-record cure & upgrade
+$50K–100K
per case moved up a tier · illustrative

The problem: a claimant with symptoms but no confirmatory diagnosis settles at Tier 1; the same claimant with a neurologist's test settles at Tier 2–3 — a multi-fold dollar gap. If that test is only requested in the final 90 days, the claimant can't schedule, complete and submit in time, and the upgrade window closes before anyone knew it was open.

What we ran: a system that scopes the whole docket, flags every record sitting one document from a tier-up, and routes those claimants to evidence collection with the runway they need — not a reminder, a manufacturing process for higher-tier settlements.

The result: same facts, higher tier, more money. Each upgrade is worth on the order of $50K–100K of case value, and a fraction of a symptomatic cohort moving up captures it fast — value that otherwise sits locked at the lower grid point. Timeliness is the lever: the more that reach the gate before it closes, the more get upgraded.

“The difference between Tier 1 and Tier 3 isn't the facts — it's the runway. We manufacture runway.”

Re-opening lost cases
≈ $500k
for roughly ten re-opened matters · illustrative

The problem: a dormant matter is a claim the firm already owns, sitting at $0 because contact or data is missing — capital parked at zero return.

What we ran: re-contacted and re-graded a handful of closed matters — a data bridge restored them to the active grid.

The result: on the order of $500k for ~10 re-opened matters. The magnitude varies by docket — the mechanism repeats: a data bridge returns a lost case to settlement value.

“Just activating ten cases gives you close to half a million. The ROI on this is astronomical.”

Claimant reach & sign-off readiness
55% → 85%
of lost claimants reachable again — the sign-off window survives

The problem: the firm had lost contact with a large share of its open matters. A settlement needs sign-off from nearly every claimant — unreachable ones are the binary risk that stalls or re-cuts the whole payout.

What we ran: a case-update bridge — a welcome text, then a monthly SMS with a check-and-confirm link, then email, then physical mail.

The result: reach-through went from 55% to 85% of claimants. Reachable claimants are matters that sign off and settle instead of dropping at $0 — and a reachable docket is a docket that closes on schedule.

“You don't pay a PI firm to find missing people at the 11th hour — you just don't lose people in the first place.”

QA cull & capital preservation
~20% → near 0%
thin or unqualified cases · $0 record spend on them

The problem: roughly one in five open files was thin, could never qualify, or was outright fraud — dead cases still burning working capital and staff hours, quietly dragging the NAV backers underwrote.

What we ran: an always-on QA loop grading every matter against the eligibility gates and closing out non-qualifiers at intake, before any work-up.

The result: the thin/unqualified share dropped to near zero, and thousands of non-qualifiers were closed with $0 medical-record spend — the drag and the cost line removed before they existed.

“We closed thousands of non-qualifying cases — and never paid for a single medical record on them.”

Settlement acceleration
+$2.5M
time-value recovered from a 6-month faster close · illustrative

The problem: even when cases eventually sign off, every month of delay is investor capital stuck at 0% — and an end-of-window scramble adds PI firms, overtime, and compressed terms.

What we ran: early, consistent engagement so sign-offs, cures, and documentation are complete before the settlement window opens. No scramble, no timeline compression.

The result: accelerating a $50M docket by six months at a 10% cost of capital recovers on the order of $2.5M in time-value. Same cases, same settlement — paid out sooner, which is worth real money.

“The only thing worse than a bad settlement is a good settlement that pays out two years too late.”

MDL sign-off protection
binary
tail-risk hedge against sign-off failure — the MDL lives or dies

The problem: a mass tort settlement requires sign-off from nearly every claimant. If 5–10% are unreachable when the window opens, the MDL can blow up — the judge walks, the defendant walks, or the settlement re-cuts at a fraction. There is no partial credit.

What we ran: proactive claimant-management infrastructure — contactability, cure tracking, and sign-off readiness scoring from day one, so the docket is sign-off-ready before the window opens.

The result: 85%+ reach shifts the collapse risk from possible to remote — the binary downside is hedged without an 11th-hour PI spend.

“You don't risk a judge deposing your firm because you couldn't locate three hundred claimants. You just don't lose people.”
