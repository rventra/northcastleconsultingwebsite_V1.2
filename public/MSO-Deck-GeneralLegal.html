Structure: Two Ways to Engage

Vendor Fees · or a Per-Case Fee Share

Option 1: MSO Agreement

Vendor Fees

Your firm retains full ownership. The MSO is a contracted vendor for docket operations, the data layer, and — if you want it — acquisition. Turn modules on or off as the docket grows.

  • Compensation: per-case operations fee.
  • Paper: Master Services Agreement; live in 30 days; 90-day stage gate before scaling.
  • Firm keeps: 100% of the firm, the client data, and independent legal judgment.

Option 2: Per-Case Fee Share

Arizona · Since 2021

Arizona eliminated ER 5.4 in 2021. An Arizona firm may share a client fee with a nonlawyer, and ER 1.5(e) does not apply to that share — the State Bar’s FAQ on the rule changes says so directly. On cases we source, the MSO takes a per-case share of the fee, paid at settlement. No equity, no ABS application on our side, no change to your ownership.

  • Compensation: origination share on cases we source, paid at settlement — aligned on each case’s outcome, not on firm-wide economics.
  • Paper: Master Services Agreement plus a per-case fee-share schedule; client disclosure where the share involves client information. Live in 30 days.
  • Firm keeps: 100% ownership of the firm, 100% of the fee on cases you originate yourself, and full control of legal judgment. Outside Arizona your state’s ER 5.4 governs, and Option 1 is the route.

Appendix B // Litigation Logistics

MDL Filing Strategy & SOL Preservation

Home Court §1407 Transfer vs. MDL Direct Filing

Path A: Home Court → §1407 Transfer

Default

File in the plaintiff's home federal district before SOL runs; the JPML sweeps the case into the MDL as a tag-along via Conditional Transfer Order.

  • No local counsel, nationwide. JPML Rule 2.1(c) lets the attorney of record follow the case into any transferee court — no pro hac vice, no local co-counsel.
  • Substantive law travels. Under Van Dusen, the transferee court applies the home state's choice-of-law rules, including its SOL and discovery rule.
  • Remand rights intact. Lexecon guarantees return to the transferor court for trial.

Path B: Direct Filing into MDL

Where CMO Allows

Many transferee judges issue Direct Filing Orders that let plaintiffs file straight into the MDL, cutting weeks of transfer paperwork.

  • Designate the originating district on the short-form complaint. It anchors choice of law (courts apply Van Dusen as if transferred) and sets the presumptive remand venue.
  • Read the CMO before relying on it. Rule 2.1(c) does not apply automatically — admission and the "No Lexecon Waiver" clause follow the order's own terms.
  • If the CMO is silent on choice of law, default to Path A.

SOL Preservation Toolkit

Controls
  • Dual docket calendar. The MSO CRM mirrors the firm's docketing system for redundant SOL tracking by originating state.
  • Don't lean on the discovery rule. In re Taxotere (5th Cir.): visible injury starts the clock before the manufacturer is identified. Latent-defect torts (3M Earplugs) fare better.
  • Tolling agreements buy time for records and causation workup — but statutes of repose generally cannot be tolled.
  • §1631 fail-safe. A jurisdictional miss transfers "in the interest of justice" and keeps the original filing date (Franco v. Mabe Trucking). Move to transfer; never accept dismissal.

  • Counsel opens the estate, appoints a representative, and executes the distribution; costs come from the client's share.

Operational Safeguards & Risk Mitigation

Protecting the Firm's License

Intake & Data Security

Top of Funnel

MSO infrastructure is built to keep compliance exposure out of the firm at the point of acquisition.

  • TCPA compliance: consent logging (Jornaya / ActiveProspect) on every digital lead to shut down class-action exposure.
  • HIPAA-secure data: intake forms and medical record repositories are encrypted and access-controlled.
  • Ethical advertising: all media copy is pre-approved by the firm for compliance with Rules 7.1 and 7.3.

Legal Firewalls

UPL Boundary

Clear operational boundaries ensure the MSO never crosses into the unauthorized practice of law.

  • Independent legal judgment: the MSO manages the business; lawyers direct case strategy. No involvement in settlement advice.
  • Scripted intake: call center staff qualify claimants using firm-approved scripts without giving legal advice.
  • Dual-calendar SOL controls: redundant, automated docketing integrations to prevent missed statutory deadlines.
